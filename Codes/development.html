7 Cures for a Lean Purse

George S. Clason spells out 7 steps to financial freedom in this chapter. The first cure is to say, “start thy purse to fattening” – that is to save at least 10% of your income. Other wise it is said to be the second: “control thy expenditures” – live below thy means. The third is to “make thy gold multiply” – invest wisely. The fourth is to “guard thy treasures from loss” – avoid risky investments. The fifth is: “make of thy dwelling a profitable investment” – own your home. The sixth is to “insure a future income” – plan for retirement. The 7th is to “increase thy ability to earn” – keep learning and improving your skill.

Reflection: I can set up these cures in my life by saving a bit more money in my allowance, watching how I spend money and investing in my education. For me, the most important lesson learned is that financial discipline begins with small consistent steps. Aki to the Jamaican saying, "Every mikkle mek a mukkle".

Jump to 5 Laws of Gold

5 Laws of Gold

The five laws are that the gold is for those who save it, that it is for the wise, that it is with the careful owner; that it escapes from the man who is not a stranger to it; that it flees from the man who is chasing too great a return.

Reflection: I learn to be patient, to seek the counsel of the wise and not to follow get-rich schemes. During my academic career, I take the same approach: regular study time, networking, and no shortcuts. That I have learned the most is that wealth (and knowledge) is built through discipline and good choices.
